MUSEUM ARCHIVE · INTERNAL MEMO

What really happened?

THE MIDNIGHT GARDEN / GALLERY FOUR
CONFIDENTIAL
FILE 014

Most visitors believed the famous original of The Midnight Garden had hung in Gallery Four for years. In fact, since 1998 the museum had displayed a painstaking copy, while the original remained in climate-controlled storage.

Last Tuesday, conservator Dr Evelyn Vale noticed a split in the copy’s wooden support and requested its immediate removal. She asked that the canvas be taken to the workshop before the next public opening. The schedule on the museum website, however, still gave Monday as the official conservation date because the communications team had not approved an earlier announcement.

At 6:12 p.m. on Friday, security officer Adam Price entered Gallery Four with a staff key. He carried a narrow wooden case containing tools and small frame fittings; two technicians later moved the wrapped canvas through a service corridor. The alarm did not sound because the removal had been authorised.

The next day, visitors encountered an empty frame and assumed there had been a robbery. No one had updated the exhibition label, and a photograph on the museum’s homepage still showed the painting in its usual place. The incident exposed a failure of communication, not a failure of security.
